A galaxy… is not just a collection of stars.

It is a cosmic city—vast, ancient, and alive in ways we are only beginning to understand.

Each galaxy contains billions, sometimes trillions, of stars. Planets orbit them. Systems form, collapse, and are reborn over millions of years. And at the very center of most galaxies… lies something unimaginable.

A supermassive black hole.

Invisible, yet powerful enough to control the motion of everything around it. It bends space, distorts time, and consumes anything that comes too close. Even light cannot escape its grip.

Our home, the Milky Way, is just one of these galaxies. It stretches across more than 100,000 light-years, holding hundreds of billions of stars. And somewhere, on the edge of one of its spiral arms… is us.

But the universe does not stand still.

Galaxies move. They collide. They merge into something new. In fact, billions of years from now, the Milky Way will collide with the Andromeda galaxy in a slow, inevitable dance of gravity.

Stars will shift. Skies will change.

But life, perhaps, will go on.

Yet the deeper mystery remains…

How did galaxies form in the first place?

After the Big Bang, matter began to gather under gravity, slowly building the first structures of the universe. Over time, these structures became galaxies—the building blocks of everything we see today.

And still, we have only scratched the surface.

There are billions of galaxies out there.

Each one holding secrets.

Each one possibly harboring life.

And somewhere, in that endless cosmic ocean…

Another civilization might be looking back—wondering the exact same thing.
