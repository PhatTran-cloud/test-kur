The universe is vast… but what's more terrifying is how little we truly understand it.

Every night, when we look up at the stars, we are not just seeing light—we are looking back in time. Some of that light has traveled for billions of years, carrying secrets from a past we may never fully uncover.

Scientists estimate that everything we can see—stars, planets, galaxies—makes up less than five percent of the universe. The rest? It's invisible. Unknown. Something we call dark matter and dark energy.

But what are they, really?

No one knows.

Dark matter doesn't emit light. It doesn't interact the way normal matter does. Yet, without it, galaxies would simply fall apart. It's the invisible glue holding the cosmos together.

And then there's dark energy… an even deeper mystery. It's pushing the universe to expand faster and faster, as if something unseen is stretching the fabric of reality itself.

But perhaps the most haunting question is this…

Are we alone?

In a universe with billions of galaxies, each containing billions of stars, the odds suggest that life should exist elsewhere. And yet, we hear nothing. No signals. No signs. Just silence.

This contradiction is known as the Fermi Paradox.

So where is everyone?

Some believe intelligent civilizations destroy themselves before they can reach the stars. Others think we simply aren't looking in the right way… or worse, we are being deliberately ignored.

The universe is not just a place of wonder—it is a place of unanswered questions.

And the deeper we explore…

The more we realize how small, and how lost, we truly are.
