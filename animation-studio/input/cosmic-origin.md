Before the first empire rose… before the first ocean formed… before Earth even had a name, the universe was already in motion.
Imagine darkness—total, endless darkness. Then, suddenly, a burst of light so powerful it changed everything. Space expanded. Time began. Matter formed. And from that first moment, the cosmos started building its masterpiece.
Now picture a sea of stars stretching beyond sight. Giant nebulae bloom like colored clouds in the void—crimson, blue, and gold—where new stars are born in silence. Some burn fiercely, brighter than entire galaxies. Others fade slowly, like candles in an endless cathedral of night.
Then come the planets. Frozen worlds with oceans hidden under ice. Gas giants wrapped in violent storms. Barren deserts under black skies. And somewhere among them, small rocky worlds orbiting distant suns—worlds that may be waiting, just as Earth once did, for life to begin.
Look closer, and the universe becomes even stranger. Black holes twist gravity into something almost unreal, bending light itself like glass. Galaxies collide in slow motion, their stars scattering like sparks in a cosmic fire. Comets drift like ancient messengers, carrying dust older than our planet.
And yet, for all its scale, the most breathtaking truth is this: you are part of it. The atoms in your body were forged inside stars that died billions of years ago. The calcium in your bones, the iron in your blood, the oxygen in your lungs—all of it came from the cosmos.
So when you look into the night sky, you are not just looking at distant lights.
You are looking at your origin.
At a story written in fire, gravity, and time.
And for one brief moment in the life of the universe…
the universe is looking back through you.
