# Cinematic Event Expansion

## Purpose

Convert short creative intent into a complete cinematic event without requiring the user to know VFX terminology.

The user may say only “massive explosion, camera shakes.” The system must infer the effect stack from event physics, scene scale, environment, narrative emphasis, camera distance, and transition context.

## Expansion algorithm

For any shorthand event, derive:

1. **Event class** — impact, combustion, destruction, launch, landing, passage, electrical, magical, fluid, weather, environmental, transformation.
2. **Scale** — micro, human, vehicle, room, building, city/landscape, cosmic/abstract.
3. **Medium/material** — concrete, steel, glass, soil, water, snow, vegetation, fabric, atmosphere, fictional energy.
4. **Energy source** — kinetic, pressure, combustion, heat, electrical, gravitational, mechanical, magical/fictional.
5. **Primary phenomenon** — the one visual event the audience must read first.
6. **Secondary consequences** — effects that prove the primary event has force and scale.
7. **Environment response** — everything around the event that should react.
8. **Camera response** — only what the camera could physically/optically experience.
9. **Lighting response** — temporary or persistent changes caused by the event.
10. **Aftermath** — persistent state that prevents the world from resetting.
11. **Transition opportunity** — effect state capable of carrying a cut.

## Camera impulse model

Never use arbitrary random shake as the main response.

Think qualitatively as:

`impact arrival -> sharp impulse -> high-frequency vibration -> lower-frequency heavy oscillation -> damped settle`

Modify by:

- distance to event;
- camera support/rig: handheld, shoulder, tripod, vehicle mount, drone, virtual crane;
- event scale;
- medium transmitting the force;
- focal length and framing;
- whether the camera itself is struck by debris, wind, water, or pressure.

Large distant events may have delayed camera/environment response. Close events may produce abrupt occlusion, debris crossings, temporary loss of framing, focus disruption, or exposure pumping.

## Explosion macro

A cinematic explosion may draw from:

- pre-event vibration, leaking fuel, pressure, sparks, dust tremor;
- white-hot/bright attack flash;
- fire/gas expansion;
- pressure front or shockwave if visible conditions support it;
- primary debris with mass-dependent trajectories;
- fine ejecta and ground dust;
- smoke body with buoyancy and wind;
- embers/sparks from hot material;
- secondary collisions and fragment bounce;
- temporary illumination and moving shadows;
- exposure adaptation and motivated bloom/glare;
- nearby cloth, foliage, cables, doors, signs, papers, puddles, windows, suspension, loose props reacting;
- camera impulse and settling;
- lingering fire/smoke, scorch, fragments, dust and changed atmosphere.

Do not automatically include every layer. Select what the environment and story justify.

## Heavy landing macro

For a heavy robot/vehicle/creature landing:

- body deceleration and structural compression;
- suspension/joint compression;
- foot/landing-pad contact deformation if material permits;
- ground dust sheet and coarse ejecta;
- pebbles/debris with ballistic motion;
- radial cracks only for brittle/weak surfaces and sufficient force;
- nearby loose objects, foliage, cables, water surfaces reacting;
- low-frequency camera impact plus smaller high-frequency vibration;
- body follow-through and secondary parts oscillating;
- dust drift and mechanical settling after contact.

## High-speed camera pass macro

For a car, aircraft, projectile, creature, or object passing near camera:

- dominant subject motion and parallax;
- wake/air displacement;
- dust, spray, leaves, papers or loose material disturbed if present;
- foreground motion streak/blur from near crossing;
- camera buffeting or operator compensation if justified;
- focus challenge/rack or brief focal loss if cinematic intent supports it;
- reflection/light sweeps across surfaces;
- trailing residue continuing after the subject exits.

## Surface impact macro

Derive the response from target material:

- concrete/stone: chips, dust, radial cracks, coarse fragments;
- metal: sparks, hot chips, dents, vibration;
- glass: crack propagation, shard detach, glitter/specular breakup, secondary falls;
- wood: splinters, fibers, dust, flex;
- soil/sand: crater/ejecta, dust, granular collapse;
- water: crown/sheet, droplets, mist, ripples, wetness.

## Launch/thrust macro

For rockets, jets, engines, or powerful exhaust:

- ignition transient;
- hot core/exhaust plume;
- turbulence and plume expansion;
- heat distortion;
- ground dust/smoke/spray displacement;
- vehicle vibration;
- local illumination when appropriate;
- camera vibration tied to scale/distance;
- plume changing with altitude/pressure/environment;
- residue or disturbed ground after departure.

## Portal / energy event macro

For fictional energy, preserve internal rules:

- charge/field buildup;
- nearby particles, dust, cloth, hair, fog or debris reacting to force field if present;
- emissive or refractive boundary;
- distortion/refraction tied to portal geometry;
- local light spill;
- volumetric or particle flow showing field direction;
- stable phase with readable depth/parallax;
- collapse/discharge phase;
- residual particles/light/heat/marking if the event was energetic.

## Restraint rule

Cinematic expansion is not “add everything.” Choose one hero effect, a small set of physically meaningful secondary effects, environment response, camera/light response when justified, and aftermath.
