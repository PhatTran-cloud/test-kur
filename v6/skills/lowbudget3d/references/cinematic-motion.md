# Cinematic motion and transitions

## Every object has a temporal state

Classify each visible object as active, reactive, passive, optical, camera-induced, or intentionally static in world space.

Motion must be motivated by story and physics. Consider force, mass, material, constraints, pivot, inertia, drag, contact and secondary motion.

Build causal chains rather than synchronized noise:

`primary action -> attached-part lag -> environmental response -> optical response -> camera compensation`

## Camera

Camera motion needs a verb: reveal, pursue, isolate, compress, destabilize, connect, transfer attention.

Maintain depth-correct parallax and coherent focus behavior.

## Scene transition

Treat A -> B as an energy transfer. Compare:

- motion vector;
- velocity phase;
- angular momentum;
- screen position;
- apparent scale;
- depth ordering;
- composition/shape;
- light/exposure;
- focus/blur;
- material flow;
- pose/action;
- sound.

Preserve or deliberately transform multiple channels. Avoid unexplained zero-velocity resets, teleportation, pose reset, cloth/particle reset, camera flips and generic effects used to hide discontinuity.
