# Cinematic Motion Recipes

These are starting structures, not presets. Adapt to story, object physics, and shot geometry.

## Luxury Product Reveal

- Product remains physically grounded.
- Camera performs a controlled arc or lateral slide.
- Highlights and reflections travel over surfaces according to camera/light geometry.
- Tiny environmental particles or haze add depth, not distraction.
- Transition through a specular highlight, foreground occluder, or matched product contour.
- Keep object motion restrained; let camera, reflections, focus, and light do most of the work.

## High-Energy Trailer Impact

- Build anticipation with a brief compression or near-still moment.
- Release hero action with acceleration.
- Camera reacts or counter-moves.
- Secondary debris/cloth/dust follows with delay.
- Cut during peak energy or transfer it through a whip/occlusion.
- Enter next shot already moving; do not restart from zero.

## Impossible Camera Passage

- Establish a clear target opening/occluder.
- Accelerate toward it while preserving parallax.
- Let the surface/foreground fill frame.
- Cut or morph while detail is hidden.
- Continue camera vector in the new environment.
- Rebuild parallax immediately so the viewer perceives continuous travel.

## Character-to-Environment Match Cut

- End on a strong limb/body arc or silhouette.
- Match the arc to architecture, machinery, landscape, or another subject in B.
- Preserve direction and approximate speed.
- Use sound overlap to bind the semantic jump.

## Mechanical Transformation

- Define hinges, rails, telescoping parts, rotating axes, and collisions before animation.
- Sequence mechanisms causally instead of moving every piece at once.
- Add actuator acceleration, contact settling, vibration, and reflected-light changes.
- Transition using a moving panel, gear, light strip, or camera passage through the mechanism.

## Atmospheric Material Bridge

- Hero action displaces smoke/dust/water/fabric.
- Let the material occupy more of the frame near the edit.
- Continue equivalent flow direction and turbulence scale in B.
- Reveal the new scene as density clears or focus resolves.

## Slow Cinematic Tension

- Keep hero movement small and deliberate.
- Use micro-motion: breathing, fingers, cloth tension, subtle focus drift, environmental movement.
- Camera drifts slowly with high inertia.
- Use sound and lighting changes to create motion without busy blocking.
- Transition via shadow, focus, foreground pass, or slow composition match rather than a flashy effect.
