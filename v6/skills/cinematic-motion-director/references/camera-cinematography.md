# Camera Cinematography

## Camera has a dramatic verb

Before choosing a move, complete: **“The camera moves in order to ____.”**

Good verbs: reveal, pursue, discover, isolate, compress, destabilize, inspect, orbit, transfer attention, enter, escape, witness, conceal.

If the answer is merely “look cinematic,” keep the camera simpler.

## Core camera behaviors

- **Dolly/push-in:** increase intimacy, pressure, discovery, or scale change.
- **Pull-out:** reveal context, isolation, consequence, or loss of control.
- **Truck/lateral track:** preserve profile motion, reveal layers, support match movement.
- **Arc/orbit:** reveal form, relationship, or dimensionality while maintaining subject attention.
- **Crane/jib/pedestal:** change hierarchy and spatial understanding.
- **Pan/tilt:** redirect attention from a fixed or near-fixed position.
- **Handheld drift:** controlled imperfection; amplitude and frequency must fit the mass of the virtual/physical rig.
- **Whip pan:** strong angular-energy transfer; best when motivated by attention or action.
- **Crash move/rapid push:** impact punctuation; requires settle or an immediate cut/occlusion to avoid feeling digital.
- **Locked-off:** lets subject/environment motion dominate and can create tension through stillness.

## Camera path rules

- Use smooth spatial curves unless the rig or event motivates a discontinuity.
- Avoid arbitrary simultaneous translation, rotation, zoom, roll, and focus changes. Pick a dominant motion and let other channels support it.
- A handheld camera still has inertia. Do not create high-frequency random shake on top of large smooth moves without reason.
- When tracking a subject, decide whether the camera **leads**, **matches**, or **lags** the subject. This changes perceived energy.

## Lens and depth behavior

Focal length is not merely zoom intensity. It changes perspective when the camera position changes, subject/background relationship, and perceived spatial compression.

Use:

- wider lenses for stronger perspective and near/far speed contrast;
- longer lenses for compression, graphic layering, and subtler parallax;
- controlled focus pulls to transfer attention;
- lens breathing only when appropriate to the intended optical feel;
- depth-of-field changes as narrative transitions, not constant decoration.

## Parallax law for visual plausibility

During camera translation, nearer layers generally move faster across the frame than farther layers. During pure rotation from the optical center, depth-based parallax is reduced. If a generated shot shows distant geometry sliding faster than nearby geometry without an independent object motion, flag it as a continuity error.

## Camera and object coordination patterns

### Lead
Camera begins moving slightly before the subject. Feels anticipatory, controlled, or fated.

### Follow
Subject initiates; camera reacts. Feels observational or responsive.

### Lock
Camera matches subject speed so the subject stabilizes while background flows. Useful for energy, pursuit, and product/vehicle work.

### Counter-move
Camera moves against the subject. Increases relative speed, tension, or reveal rate.

### Orbit with internal motion
As camera arcs, allow subject pose, highlights, reflections, cloth, and background occlusion to evolve. Do not rotate the camera around a frozen render.

## Start/end frame design

Treat the final 10–20% of a shot as preparation for the transition. Shape the camera's outgoing vector, occlusion, focus, exposure, or composition so shot B can inherit it.
