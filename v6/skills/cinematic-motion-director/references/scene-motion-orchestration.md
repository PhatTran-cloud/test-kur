# Scene Motion Orchestration

## Build causal waves, not synchronized animation

A convincing scene behaves like a network of causes and delayed responses.

Example:

1. vehicle accelerates;
2. suspension squats;
3. body pitches;
4. loose antenna bends;
5. tires disturb dust;
6. dust trails and expands;
7. nearby cloth receives airflow later;
8. reflections sweep across body panels as camera tracks;
9. camera slightly lags, then catches up.

The offsets create weight and causality.

## Timing layers

Use at least three timing scales when a scene is rich:

- **primary beat:** the main action measured in large fractions of the shot;
- **secondary reaction:** delayed by a small fraction of the primary action;
- **micro/ambient behavior:** continuous, irregular, low-amplitude motion.

Avoid making every element hit the same ease curve and duration.

## Anticipation, action, follow-through, settle

For important actions, consider four phases:

1. **Anticipation** — a small preparation or camera cue.
2. **Action** — energy release.
3. **Follow-through** — attached/secondary elements continue.
4. **Settle** — energy decays or transfers into the next shot.

A transition can replace the settle: instead of stopping, transfer the remaining energy into shot B.

## Motion contrast

Cinematic scenes gain clarity through contrast:

- slow camera + fast subject;
- fast camera + controlled subject;
- still architecture + active atmosphere;
- high-energy foreground + calm background;
- smooth primary movement + small irregular secondary motion.

## Environmental response

Whenever a hero object moves, ask what else should respond:

- airflow;
- dust/debris;
- cloth/hair;
- reflections;
- shadows;
- water;
- foliage;
- suspended objects;
- practical lights;
- surface vibration;
- sound and reverb.

Do not add all reactions at full strength. Select those supported by the scene.

## Attention choreography

At any moment, decide where the viewer should look. Use motion onset, contrast, focus, light, and camera framing to pass attention from one object to the next. Too many equal onsets divide attention and make the scene feel procedural.

## Motion entropy budget

Classify each object's motion predictability:

- **low entropy:** rigid mechanical rail, locked camera, precise typography;
- **medium entropy:** human gesture, handheld drift, cloth;
- **high entropy:** smoke, sparks, dust, turbulent fluid.

Do not make every layer high entropy. Anchor chaotic materials with stable geometry or a clear camera trajectory.
