# Transition Continuity System

## Transition = energy transfer

For each boundary A -> B, build a continuity bridge. Record the final state of A and the initial state of B.

### Continuity channels

1. **Vector:** left/right/up/down/toward/away movement.
2. **Velocity:** speed and whether motion is accelerating, cruising, decelerating, or settling.
3. **Angular momentum:** pan/orbit/roll direction and speed.
4. **Screen position:** where the attention anchor exits and enters.
5. **Scale:** apparent subject size and rate of scale change.
6. **Depth:** near/mid/far layer relationship and occlusion order.
7. **Shape/composition:** silhouette, line, horizon, circle, frame-within-frame, negative space.
8. **Light/exposure:** direction, luminance, flare, darkness, color temperature.
9. **Focus:** focal plane, blur amount, bokeh character.
10. **Material flow:** smoke, liquid, fabric, particles, dust, reflection.
11. **Pose/action:** match-on-action or continuation of body/object gesture.
12. **Sound:** transient hit, riser, ambience, dialogue, mechanical sound, whoosh.

For premium seamless transitions, preserve or intentionally transform at least two channels; three or more is preferred.

## Transition families

### Motion carry-through

Shot A ends with a clear vector and velocity. Shot B begins with compatible motion before the viewer can perceive a reset. Useful for vehicles, character movement, camera tracks, thrown objects, doors, mechanical arms, and typography.

### Occlusion cut

A foreground object, body, wall, vehicle, cloth, shadow, darkness, smoke, or prop fills enough of the frame to conceal the cut. Match the occluder's velocity and edge direction across the cut.

### Whip bridge

Accelerate into a fast pan/arc so detail becomes directional blur. Cut during the blur, then enter B with the same angular direction and a believable deceleration or continued speed. Do not whip to a dead-static first frame.

### Shape/composition match

Align dominant geometry between scenes: circle -> wheel -> eye; horizontal horizon -> table edge; silhouette -> architecture; doorway -> screen. Motion can reinforce the match by rotating, scaling, or translating the shared shape.

### Focus bridge

Defocus at the end of A, cut near maximal blur, then resolve focus in B. Preserve blur direction/amount and avoid a sudden crisp first frame.

### Light/exposure bridge

Use a practical light, flare, blown highlight, shadow, blackout, emissive panel, or passing reflection to bridge exposure. The next shot must inherit plausible brightness and direction before settling.

### Material-flow bridge

Let smoke, dust, water, cloth, particles, sparks, glass reflections, or ink occupy the frame and continue similar flow into B. Preserve direction, turbulence scale, density progression, and acceleration.

### Camera passage

Move through a doorway, behind a pillar, through darkness, beneath a surface, past a body, or extremely close to an object. The passage itself becomes the edit point.

### Object handoff

A moving object exits or dominates A and a visually/semantically related object inherits its screen path in B. Great for product films and narrative montage.

## Anti-jank boundary checklist

Before approving a transition, compare A_end and B_start:

- motion vector differs by less than the intended dramatic change;
- subject/object does not teleport to an unrelated screen location;
- apparent scale change is motivated by camera/lens or hidden by occlusion;
- camera rotation does not flip without a cut motivation;
- motion blur survives the edit appropriately;
- cloth/hair/particles do not reset;
- reflection and highlight direction is plausible;
- background parallax matches camera direction;
- first frames of B already contain motion when A exits with high energy;
- sound either bridges or intentionally punctuates the cut.

## Hard cuts can still be cinematic

A seamless transition is not always correct. Use a hard cut when the script demands rupture, impact, surprise, temporal jump, geographic contrast, or rhythm. Even then, preserve intentional graphic, action, sound, or eye-trace logic when possible.
